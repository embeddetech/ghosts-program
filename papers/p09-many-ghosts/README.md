## Abstract

**Draft — not for circulation.** The quantum interpretation debate is usually left to philosophy because the leading readings are believed empirically equivalent. This paper argues that the equivalence is conditional on the matter content of the world, and makes three claims of decreasing modesty about what the indivisible-stochastic-process reconstruction of quantum theory, and the ghost sectors studied in its companion papers, say to the Everett interpretation. *First*, Barandes’ reconstruction (the wave function as nomic, law-like bookkeeping for an indivisible stochastic process on configurations) removes the *no-alternative* argument for $`\psi`$-ontology without refuting it, and the Bender–Mannheim program (the Hilbert-space inner product derived from the operator algebra rather than postulated) sharpens the resulting underdetermination by dissolving a second postulated primitive; the Pusey–Barrett–Rudolph theorem does not apply, by the theorem’s own scope—an exit we take from Barandes and cite rather than establish, so this first claim is conditional on its soundness; the exit’s real load is the nomic reclassification of $`\psi`$. *Second*, the ghost trilemma of the companion papers—coupled ghosts, empirical Lorentz invariance, definite configurations: any two, never all three, at tree level—*locates* Everett as the drop-definite-configurations horn; this is a location, not a criticism, since manifest covariance is exactly what dropping configurations buys. *Third*—the paper’s own claim, and it is conjectural—a coupled ghost sector would separate the two ontological classes *observationally with the Born rule intact*: a beables ontology is foliation-anchored, the foliation’s determinacy is a measurable property of the state that is generically nonzero on ordinary matter but whose observable manifestation is shielded there, and a ghost sector would (conditionally) unshield it through a frame-locked kinematic dipole and spectral edge, while no-preferred-slicing ontologies do not entail such a signal, remaining compatible with either outcome. The claim rides on the converse of a no-signaling shield proven only for a finite core, and it discriminates a class (foliation-anchored single-world versus no-slicing), not a single interpretation. It separates the classes cleanly only when the ghost Lagrangian is held fixed and Lorentz-invariant and the individuating frame arises solely as a state-functional foliation: a fundamentally Lorentz-violating completion, or a birth-surface (cosmological initial-condition) structure, is available to a no-slicing interpreter too and would reproduce the same signal—the birth surface at no cost, the Lorentz-violating completion at the price of manifest covariance—so a positive detection is merely *consistent with* the foliation-anchored class—being confounded by such completions or initial conditions—while the null direction is the cleaner discriminator, because the beables class *entails* the signal (of the computed template amplitude, for an established sector, under the assumed comoving-frame identification—a realization off that frame, below the template’s signal fraction, or individuated by a completion other than the template’s frame cutoff, survives the pointed null) whereas a no-slicing ontology merely *permits* it. In fact no purely state-functional rescue of a ghost vacuum has yet been exhibited, so on current knowledge every concrete realization of the positive signal lies in the confounded branch and the clean case is conjectural over and above the shield-converse conjecture; this only strengthens the null direction, since then every viable beables completion predicts a signal—at the template’s sensitivity, for a template-saturating realization. The closest ancestor of the escape, Valentini’s quantum non-equilibrium, requires Born-rule violations; here the discriminator is a sector, not a rule failure. (One other equilibrium route to foliation observability, via contested arrival-time observables, is disjoint from ours.) Along the way we state the bookkeeping objection an Everettian should raise against the stochastic reconstruction—to our knowledge for the first time in print—and give a partly conditional answer.
